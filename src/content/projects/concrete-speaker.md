  <h2 class="font-display text-2xl font-bold text-[var(--text-primary)] !my-0">The Problem & Multi-Objective Brief</h2>
</div>

<p class="text-base leading-relaxed opacity-90">
  I wanted desktop speakers. I also needed a monitor riser to fix my eye-level ergonomics. And I wanted something on my desk that felt like it was actually designed - not bought off a shelf and forgotten.
</p>

<p class="text-base leading-relaxed opacity-90">
  Most commercial solutions solved only one of these requirements in isolation, creating desk clutter and disjointed materials. So the core question became: <strong>what if the speakers, the monitor riser, and the aesthetic statement were the same object?</strong>
</p>

<div class="grid grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-3 gap-4 my-6">
  <div class="p-4 border border-[var(--border)] bg-[var(--bg-surface)] rounded flex flex-col justify-between">
    <div class="font-mono text-xs uppercase text-[var(--amber)] font-bold mb-2">01. Ergonomics</div>
    <div class="font-display font-semibold text-[var(--text-primary)] text-sm mb-1">Eye-Level Riser</div>
    <p class="text-xs opacity-80 leading-relaxed">Elevates the display to eliminate cervical strain and creates an open bridge for desk storage.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="p-4 border border-[var(--border)] bg-[var(--bg-surface)] rounded flex flex-col justify-between">
    <div class="font-mono text-xs uppercase text-[var(--royal)] font-bold mb-2">02. Acoustics</div>
    <div class="font-display font-semibold text-[var(--text-primary)] text-sm mb-1">Directional Projection</div>
    <p class="text-xs opacity-80 leading-relaxed">Angled baffle geometry aims high-frequency drivers directly at listener ear level while damping resonance.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="p-4 border border-[var(--border)] bg-[var(--bg-surface)] rounded flex flex-col justify-between">
    <div class="font-mono text-xs uppercase text-[var(--crimson)] font-bold mb-2">03. Aesthetics</div>
    <div class="font-display font-semibold text-[var(--text-primary)] text-sm mb-1">Brutalist Centerpiece</div>
    <p class="text-xs opacity-80 leading-relaxed">Raw cast stone texture paired with organic warm wood grain for an architectural presence.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="p-4 rounded border-l-4 border-l-[var(--amber)] bg-[var(--bg-surface)] border border-[var(--border)] my-6">
  <div class="font-mono text-xs text-[var(--amber)] font-bold uppercase mb-1">Core Constraint</div>
  <p class="text-sm opacity-90 mb-0">
    No access to a workshop, CNC machinery, or power tools. The entire design had to be engineered around accessible local materials, manual blade cutting, and hand-cast concrete.
  </p>
</div>

  <h2 class="font-display text-2xl font-bold text-[var(--text-primary)] !my-0">Material Reasoning & Acoustic Architecture</h2>
</div>

<p class="text-base leading-relaxed opacity-90">
  The material selection was driven by acoustic behavior and mechanical requirements rather than pure decoration:
</p>

<div class="grid grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-3 gap-6 my-6">
  <div class="p-5 border border-[var(--border)] bg-[var(--bg-surface)] rounded-lg space-y-2">
    <div class="font-mono text-xs uppercase text-[var(--amber)] font-bold">Structural Housing</div>
    <h3 class="font-display text-lg font-bold text-[var(--text-primary)] !mt-0 !mb-1">Cast Concrete</h3>
    <p class="text-xs md:text-sm opacity-85 leading-relaxed">
      Dense, heavy, and porous. Its substantial mass dampens cabinet vibration transmitted through the housing, resisting unwanted acoustic coloration and projecting acoustic energy directly forward.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="p-5 border border-[var(--border)] bg-[var(--bg-surface)] rounded-lg space-y-2">
    <div class="font-mono text-xs uppercase text-[var(--royal)] font-bold">Mounting Interface</div>
    <h3 class="font-display text-lg font-bold text-[var(--text-primary)] !mt-0 !mb-1">Hardwood Veneer</h3>
    <p class="text-xs md:text-sm opacity-85 leading-relaxed">
      Used for the front driver baffle. Provides clean machinability for precise driver cutouts, mechanical damping under continuous cone oscillation, and rich visual contrast against gray concrete.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="p-5 border border-[var(--border)] bg-[var(--bg-surface)] rounded-lg space-y-2">
    <div class="font-mono text-xs uppercase text-[var(--crimson)] font-bold">Internal Cavity</div>
    <h3 class="font-display text-lg font-bold text-[var(--text-primary)] !mt-0 !mb-1">Cotton Batting</h3>
    <p class="text-xs md:text-sm opacity-85 leading-relaxed">
      Internal acoustic damping layer installed inside the sealed enclosure cavity. Absorbs high-velocity air reflections to suppress internal standing waves and prevent muddy lower-mid frequencies.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

      </div>
      <span class="font-mono text-xs px-2.5 py-1 border border-[var(--border)] bg-[var(--bg)] rounded text-[var(--crimson)]">Hand Fabrication</span>
    </div>
    <p class="text-sm md:text-base leading-relaxed opacity-90">
      Without access to CNC milling, the CAD model had to be translated into physical molds manually. Foam board sheets were hand-measured, cut with a utility knife, and assembled with all-purpose contact adhesive and hot glue to construct multi-part negative casting molds with internal core inserts.
    </p>

        <h3 class="font-display text-xl font-bold text-[var(--text-primary)] !my-0">Casting, Curing & Final Assembly</h3>
      </div>
      <span class="font-mono text-xs px-2.5 py-1 border border-[var(--border)] bg-[var(--bg)] rounded text-[var(--amber)]">Casting & Joinery</span>
    </div>
    <p class="text-sm md:text-base leading-relaxed opacity-90">
      Mixed and poured high-density concrete into the mold cavities, agitating manually to release trapped air voids. After de-molding, the raw seam marks and surface textures were intentionally preserved to celebrate the brutalist fabrication process. Fitted the internal cotton damping, mounted and sealed the full-range drivers onto the hardwood front baffles, wired the enclosures, and joined the wooden bridge structure.
    </p>

  <h2 class="font-display text-2xl font-bold text-[var(--text-primary)] !my-0">Retrospective: What Worked vs. What Didn't</h2>
</div>

<div class="grid grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-2 gap-6 my-6">

  <!-- Worked -->
  <div class="p-5 border border-emerald-900/50 bg-[var(--bg-surface)] rounded-lg space-y-3">
    <div class="font-mono text-xs uppercase text-emerald-400 font-bold flex items-center gap-1.5">
      <span>✓</span> Engineered Successes
    </div>
    <ul class="space-y-2 text-sm opacity-90">
      <li><strong>Unified Form Factor:</strong> Consolidating speakers, riser, and cable bridge into one object eliminated desk clutter permanently.</li>
      <li><strong>Vibration Damping:</strong> The high-density concrete enclosure effectively prevents desk resonance and chassis vibration during loud playback.</li>
      <li><strong>Acoustic Clarity:</strong> Internal cotton damping prevents hollow, boomy cavity resonance in the lower-mid spectrum.</li>
      <li><strong>Daily Reliability:</strong> Serves as my primary daily desktop workstation centerpiece.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <!-- Didn't Work -->
  <div class="p-5 border border-[var(--crimson)]/40 bg-[var(--bg-surface)] rounded-lg space-y-3">
    <div class="font-mono text-xs uppercase text-[var(--crimson)] font-bold flex items-center gap-1.5">
      <span>✕</span> Limitations & Honest Gaps
    </div>
    <ul class="space-y-2 text-sm opacity-90">
      <li><strong>Unfinished Storage Drawer:</strong> The sliding under-bridge drawer planned in the Fusion 360 model was left unexecuted due to hand-tooling limits.</li>
      <li><strong>Enclosure Volume Tuning:</strong> Cavity volume was dimensioned by ergonomics and eye rather than calculated Thiele-Small parameter optimization.</li>
      <li><strong>Mold Seam Imperfections:</strong> Hand-cut foam boards introduced slight seam variances during concrete expansion.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
